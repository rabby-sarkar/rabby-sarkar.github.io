Ship design project · NAME-300 · Mar 2024 to Jun 2025Team project

130 TEU inland container vessel, Dhaka to Khulna

A complete design spiral for a river container ship on a 354 km inland route, from owner's requirements to lines plan, structure, machinery and intact stability.

The owner asked for 130 TEU at 10 knots. Route data from BIWTA set a maximum draft of 4 m and a 25 m air draft under the lowest bridge. We compared three methods for the preliminary particulars (geosim, cube root and empirical) and settled on a 78 m hull.

I worked on the hull form in Maxsurf and Rhino, the lines plan and general arrangement in AutoCAD, Holtrop and Mennen resistance, propeller and shaft sizing, scantlings and the midship section, and the lightship weight estimate.

Stability was checked in Maxsurf for five load cases: lightship, ballast departure and arrival, and full load departure and arrival, with GZ curves and large-angle stability for each.

Calculations covered: principal particulars · offset table · lightweight · resistance & power · propeller · main engine · shaft · rudder · engine foundation · scantlings

Structural Analysis of Ship Hulls Using Finite Element Methods

Rabby Sarkar · International Journal of Applied Sciences (IJAS), Vol. 1, Issue 4

A review of finite element analysis for ship hulls under hydrostatic, hydrodynamic, wind and wave loads. It covers modelling in ANSYS Workbench and APDL, how structural integrity is assessed, where current practice falls short, and what that means for hull design.

Thesis & published research · 2026Supervisor: Dr S M Ikhtiar Mahmud

Analysis of fairlead position and heave of a spar floating wind turbine

I designed a 15 m diameter, 85 m draft spar FOWT for the Bay of Bengal, built the AQWA hydrodynamic model, and ran 3-hour time-domain simulations with Python and Capytaine BEM to find whether mooring fairlead depth controls heave.

Spar FOWT · 5 MW class · 320 m depthheave ζ₃ = +0.00 m
Illustrative motion, not simulation output

Off Bangladesh the continental shelf drops beyond 60 m within 160 km of shore. I sized a spar for a 5 MW turbine at a 320 m deep site (20.09° N, 91.50° E), with a 15 m diameter hull, 85 m draft and 15 m freeboard, ballasted with magnetite and seawater. I validated the hydrostatics in ANSYS AQWA.

I then built a frequency-domain model in Python with hydrodynamic coefficients from Capytaine BEM and a catenary mooring solver. I ran nonlinear time-domain simulations with the fairlead at five depths (34–66.5 m below the waterline), holding anchors and all other parameters fixed, over five random wave seeds.

The fairlead depth barely affects heave. Moving it from above the centre of buoyancy to below the centre of gravity changes the heave standard deviation by less than 0.1% with the original mooring, and 0.7–2% with a stiffer one. The catenary mooring supplies only 0.1–0.9% of the heave stiffness, so the vertical fairlead forces are too weak to compete with hydrostatic restoring. Heave must be controlled through the hull—a heave plate or deeper draft.
